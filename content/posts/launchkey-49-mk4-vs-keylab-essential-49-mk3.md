The Novation Launchkey 49 MK4 and the Arturia KeyLab Essential 49 mk3 are the two 49-key controllers most people end up choosing between. They cost about the same, they're the same size, and both promise to "integrate" with your DAW.

They are not the same keyboard. One is built to drive Ableton Live from the keys. The other is built to make finding and playing sounds easy in whatever software you use.

I play the Launchkey 49 MK4 in my live rig, where it handles note input next to a Push 3 and a Launchpad Pro. I haven't owned the KeyLab Essential, so that side of this comparison comes from Arturia's specs and the published reviews, not my hands.

---

## The Short Answer

- **Get the Launchkey 49 MK4** if you use Ableton Live, want pads you can actually play, or want to drive hardware synths without a computer.
- **Get the KeyLab Essential 49 mk3** if you switch between DAWs, live inside Arturia's Analog Lab sounds, or want the bigger screen for less money.

If you're on Ableton, the Launchkey is the easier call. If you're not, it's genuinely close.

## Keys: Neither Is a Piano

Both keybeds are synth-style actions that try to feel a little heavier than a toy. Novation calls the Launchkey's keys semi-weighted; Arturia describes a hybrid synth-piano feel.

I like the Launchkey's keybed. It's fully functional for bass lines, pad parts and chords, and it never gets in the way. My one wish is that it were weighted. If you're learning piano, a weighted 88 is a different purchase, and nothing in this price class replaces one.

Neither keyboard lists aftertouch on the keys. On both, pressure expression lives in the pads.

---

## Pads: The Biggest Real Difference

This is where the Launchkey pulls ahead.

The Launchkey gives you **16 pads with polyphonic aftertouch**. Each pad reads its own pressure, so finger-drumming and playing chords on the pads feel expressive, and they double as step-sequencer and clip controls in Ableton.

The KeyLab Essential has **8 pressure-sensitive pads** across two banks. That's workable for triggering drums and loops, but it's half the playing surface at once.

In my rig I barely touch the Launchkey's pads: Push 3 handles finger drumming and the Launchpad Pro launches scenes. What I use constantly on the Launchkey are the faders. If this is your only controller, though, those 16 pads are the biggest reason to pick it.

---

## Knobs, Faders and the Screen

Both give you nine faders, which in Ableton means eight tracks plus a master.

The Launchkey's knobs are **endless encoders**, so they pick up a parameter where it is instead of jumping to wherever the knob happens to be pointing. The KeyLab Essential gives you one more knob and a clickable main encoder that makes browsing quicker.

The KeyLab's **2.5" LCD** is the nicer screen, and it's the heart of how that keyboard wants you to work: scroll through Analog Lab presets, see names and categories, play. The Launchkey's OLED is small. It shows you the parameter you're touching and the mode you're in, and that's about it.

## DAW Integration: Pick Your Ecosystem

**Launchkey 49 MK4:** Built for Ableton Live first. Transport, mixer, device control, clip launching and the sequencer all map out of the box on a Mac. Logic, Cubase and Reason have scripts; anything else works over HUI. You can remap controls in Novation's Components app.

**KeyLab Essential 49 mk3:** Ships with scripts for Ableton, Logic, FL Studio, Cubase and Bitwig, and its tightest integration is with Arturia's own Analog Lab and V Collection.

If Ableton is your home, the Launchkey behaves like part of the program. I run it alongside Push 3 in the same Live set and it runs clean, with each surface assigned its own slot in Live's MIDI preferences.

## Away From the Computer

Both have a 5-pin MIDI out, so both can play a hardware synth directly.

The Launchkey goes further. Its scale modes, chord modes and arpeggiator work when you're driving hardware, so it's a usable performance keyboard for a synth rack or Eurorack setup. The KeyLab Essential can play hardware too, but its smarts are aimed at software.

The KeyLab Essential has the more flexible pedal input: sustain, expression or a footswitch. The Launchkey has a sustain input.

## What's in the Box

**Launchkey 49 MK4:** Ableton Live 12 Lite, instruments and effects from GForce, Klevgrand and Orchestral Tools, and Melodics lessons.

**KeyLab Essential 49 mk3:** Analog Lab V, Ableton Live Lite, The Gentleman upright piano, UVI Model D grand piano, two months of Loopcloud and Melodics.

Arturia's bundle is the stronger one if you don't already own instruments. Analog Lab alone is a deep library of playable synth and keys sounds.

## Bottom Line

For Ableton users, the Launchkey 49 MK4 is the better instrument. The pads and the integration are worth the small premium.

For everyone else, the KeyLab Essential 49 mk3 is the better starting point, mostly because of Analog Lab and the broader DAW support.
